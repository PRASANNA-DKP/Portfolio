PROJECT 01

ZAFI - 2.0

5.5 FT · WHEEL-BASED HUMANOID · PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Zafi 2.0 is a 5.5-foot wheel-based humanoid robot developed as a product-oriented robotics project. I worked across the complete development process, from mechanical design and fabrication to electronics, embedded control, wireless communication, assembly and system integration.

The robot combines a differential-drive mobile platform with a 5-DOF upper-body mechanism consisting of a neck, two shoulder joints and two elbow joints.

MY ROLE

I worked on the robot from concept and mechanical design through fabrication, electronics integration, embedded programming, assembly, testing and troubleshooting.

  • Mechanical design using Fusion 360
  • 3D-printed mechanical joints and components
  • CO₂ laser-cut structural components
  • Electronics integration and wiring
  • ESP32 wireless communication
  • Custom joystick controller development
  • Robot assembly and system integration
  • Testing, troubleshooting and servicing

MECHANICAL DESIGN & FABRICATION

Major mechanical components were designed in Fusion 360. Several joints and structural components were designed as modular parts so that the robot could be assembled, disassembled and serviced more easily.

3D printing was used for several custom components including neck and shoulder-related mechanisms. Structural parts were fabricated using a CO₂ laser cutting machine.

The outer body was developed using foam sheet and leather, with silicone-based finishing used to create the final humanoid appearance.

DEGREES OF FREEDOM

The upper body provides 5 degrees of freedom:

  • 1 × Neck
  • 1 × Left Shoulder
  • 1 × Right Shoulder
  • 1 × Left Elbow
  • 1 × Right Elbow

ELECTRONICS & CONTROL

  • ESP32 wireless communication
  • Arduino Mega controller
  • 5 × 25 kg-cm servo motors
  • Cytron 10 A motor drivers
  • 2 × 12 V 100 RPM geared motors
  • 12 V 10 Ah battery
  • Buck converter
  • Voltage and current indicator
  • LED strip lighting

CUSTOM JOYSTICK CONTROLLER

I designed the custom joystick controller housing using Fusion 360 and assembled the controller using a dot PCB, joystick module, push buttons and ESP32.

The controller uses ESP32 wireless communication and achieved a practical operating range of approximately 60–90 metres during testing.

  • ESP32 transmitter
  • Joystick module
  • 12 push buttons
  • Custom Fusion 360 enclosure
  • Dot PCB based electronics assembly

FACE DISPLAY & COMPUTER VISION

A 7-inch display was integrated into the robot's face along with a Raspberry Pi 5 and webcam. A face-recognition system was developed as part of the robot's interactive front-end.

PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT

The robot was developed with a product-oriented approach, focusing on modular assembly, serviceability, fabrication repeatability and overall appearance.

The approximate prototype development cost was ₹60,000–₹70,000. A planned commercial selling price was around ₹1.5–₹1.6 lakh.

The platform is also being considered for future AMR integration and further autonomous capabilities.
